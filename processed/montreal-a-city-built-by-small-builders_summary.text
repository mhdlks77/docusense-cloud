**Summary:**

This thesis by David B. Hanna examines how housing was developed in Montreal between 1866 and 1880, a time when the city was rapidly transitioning from a mercantile to an industrial economy. Using building permit records as primary data, the study details the shift in housing types, focusing particularly on the emergence and dominance of British-influenced multi-family dwellings like duplexes and triplexes. It analyzes the spatial division of the housing market between single-family houses and flats, and reveals that most housing was built by small-scale French-Canadian and some British-Canadian artisans, entrepreneurs, and shopkeepers, with very little involvement from the Irish population. Large-scale or corporate developers played only a minor role. The research also explores the methods of financing home construction through notarial records and government statutes, highlighting the significant but precarious roles of building societies, mortgage lenders, and private investors. Overall, the study shows how Montreal's distinctive built environment and class neighborhoods were shaped primarily by numerous small-scale builders and local financing arrangements during a crucial period of urban and economic transformation.
